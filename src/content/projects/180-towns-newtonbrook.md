The only new freehold low-rise tracked on this site inside the **City of Toronto**, and that location changes the closing-cost arithmetic more than anything else on the page.

Toronto charges a **municipal land transfer tax on top of the provincial one**, at broadly the same rates, so you pay it twice. On a $1,449,990 home that is roughly **$25,500 provincially plus $25,500 municipally, about $51,000 in cash on closing**. It cannot be financed into the mortgage. [Full rates](/faq/land-transfer-tax-new-home-ontario).

The deposit is the other thing to go in with your eyes open about. **Six payments of $20,000 and then the balance to 15% on occupancy** is roughly $217,000 on the largest plan. [Tarion's freehold deposit protection caps at $100,000](/faq/is-my-deposit-protected-freehold-ontario), so well over $100,000 would sit outside warranty coverage. Fifteen per cent is high for freehold; ten is the norm. This is a conversation to have with your lawyer before signing, not after.

Prices are quoted net of the enhanced rebate. The band straddles **$1,000,000**, so an entry plan gets the full 8% Ontario rebate while the top plan is capped at the flat $80,000 and has almost no federal rebate left. [How the bands work](/faq/ontario-enhanced-new-housing-rebate).

## Pricing and your rebate position

Listed prices run **$929,990 to $1,449,990** across 8 floor plans. Assuming you qualify, here is what the current programs are worth at each end:

| | At $929,990 | At $1,449,990 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario ENHR (no first-time buyer test) | **$74,399** | **$80,000** |
| Federal rebate (first-time buyers only) | **$46,499** | **$5,001** |

**Read the price list carefully.** The advertised prices are stated net of the enhanced HST rebate, meaning they assume you qualify and assign the rebate to the vendor at closing. If you do not qualify, or the rebate is reduced, you are responsible for the difference at closing in certified funds. [How net-of-rebate pricing works](/faq/price-net-of-hst-rebate-meaning).
