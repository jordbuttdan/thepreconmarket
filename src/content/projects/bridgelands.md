Bridgelands is the largest new freehold release in Woodbridge this year, and the structure of it is unusual: **three builders releasing into a single master-planned community** at the same time. Arista Homes, DECO Homes and OPUS Homes are each bringing their own product to Weston Road and Teston Road, which means one community, one set of amenities, and three different price lists, specification levels and deposit structures to compare.

That is an advantage if you know what you are comparing and a liability if you do not.

## What is being released

**Detached homes** on lots of **26, 32, 38 and 42 feet**, in bungalow, two-storey and three-storey designs, with one-car and two-car garages. OPUS is bringing rear-lane detached on the 26 and 32 foot lots as well as conventional 38 and 42 foot product.

**Townhomes** in three formats, **rear-lane**, **laneway**, and **back-to-back**, with end, corner and interior units recorded across the plans.

Everything is **freehold**. Note that master-planned communities of this type frequently involve a POTL arrangement for private laneways and shared blocks; if your agreement mentions a parcel of tied land, that carries a small monthly fee and a legal wrinkle worth raising with your lawyer immediately. [What a POTL is](/faq/potl-common-elements-condo-fees).

Current inventory shows **179 floor plans** from **1,468 to 3,623 sq ft**, weighted heavily to four-bedroom designs, 131 of the 179, against 45 three-bedroom and 3 two-bedroom plans. This is a family community, and the plan mix says so plainly.

## Location

Weston and Teston puts you in north Woodbridge, close to the Kleinburg edge, with Highway 400 and Highway 427 both reachable and Vaughan Mills and the VMC to the south.

Be realistic about what that means day to day. This is a car-dependent location at launch, as nearly all new low-rise is. The retail and services that make a community walkable arrive years after the homes do, and sometimes they arrive differently than the plan suggests. If you work downtown and do not want to drive to a GO station, weigh that honestly now rather than in 2030.

## The rebate position, and why the launch date matters

This is the most consequential thing about buying here in November 2026.

Bridgelands sits inside the **Ontario Enhanced New Housing Rebate window**, which requires an agreement of purchase and sale signed between **April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027**. Signing date, not closing date. [The deadline](/faq/hst-rebate-deadline-march-2027).

Launch pricing runs **$711,496 to $1,724,990**, and that band crosses every line in the rebate structure. Here is what the two ends are actually worth:

| | Entry town at $711,496 | 42 ft detached at $1,724,990 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario ENHR (no first-time buyer test) | **$56,919** | **$44,001** |
| Federal rebate (first-time buyers only) | **$35,574** | **$0** |

The entry product sits **below $1,000,000**, which is the best position in the whole structure: the full 8% provincially and the full 5% federally for a first-time buyer.

Everything between **$1,000,000 and $1,500,000** gets the **flat $80,000** ENHR while the federal rebate tapers to zero across that band. At $1,449,990, a first-time buyer's federal rebate is down to about **$5,000**.

Above **$1,500,000** the federal rebate is **zero** and the ENHR itself starts tapering toward the $24,000 standard, reaching zero at $1,850,000. The largest 42 foot plans are in that zone.

Two practical consequences. First, if you have **owned a home before, you are still eligible for the ENHR**, it has no first-time buyer test, and move-up buyers routinely write themselves off for no reason. [Read this one](/faq/do-i-have-to-be-first-time-buyer-ontario-rebate).

Second, **lot premiums and upgrades count toward your price**, and the published prices here exclude both. A 38 foot plan listed at $1,449,990 is roughly $50,000 of premiums and upgrades away from $1,500,000, where the federal rebate goes to zero. Crossing that line costs more than the upgrades are worth. Model your all-in number before the décor appointment, not after. [The price cliffs](/faq/hst-rebate-over-1-5-million).

[Run your own numbers](/hst-rebate/checker).

## The deposits, and the part nobody mentions

The published deposit totals are **$80,000 on townhomes**, **$110,000** on 26 and 32 foot rear-lane detached and DECO's courtyard detached, **$150,000** on 38 foot detached and **$170,000** on 42 foot detached. Arista has not published its terms yet.

Here is the thing to understand before you write the cheque. **Tarion protects a freehold deposit up to 10% of the price, capped at $100,000.** So:

- On an **entry townhome at $711,496 with $80,000 down**, Tarion covers about **$71,150**, so roughly **$8,850** is not covered.
- On a **38 foot detached at $150,000**, roughly **$50,000 sits outside** that coverage.
- On a **42 foot detached at $170,000**, roughly **$70,000 does.**

This is not a prediction about these builders, all three are long-established. It is simply what the warranty does and does not reach, and it is the kind of thing your lawyer should be addressing in the agreement rather than you discovering it later. [How deposit protection works](/faq/is-my-deposit-protected-freehold-ontario).

## What to sort out now

At this stage, preparation is most of the advantage:

- **Register for representation.** Most builders require your broker to be registered **at or before your first visit** for it to count. [Why this matters](/faq/do-i-need-a-realtor-pre-construction).
- **Have your deposit draft ready.** The first instalment is normally a bank draft on signing, at the sales office.
- **Decide your lot priorities in writing**, width, orientation, walkout grade, what is behind it. You choose in minutes, not days. [How to choose a lot](/guides/choosing-a-lot-new-low-rise).
- **Brief a real estate lawyer** so a same-day agreement review is a phone call. There is **no cooling-off period on freehold in Ontario**, the agreement binds on acceptance, unless your lot is a POTL, where a 10-day right may apply. [This is the one to understand](/faq/cooling-off-period-freehold-ontario).
- **Ask for a cap on development charges and levies.** DECO is offering levies capped at **$0** at launch. That is the most valuable single term on the incentive sheet for a closing three or four years out, and it is worth asking the other two builders to match. [Why](/faq/development-charges-levies-cap).

## Comparing the three builders

Because Arista, DECO and OPUS are selling side by side, you can and should compare:

- **Base specification**, what is actually included before upgrades. The schedules differ. [What's usually excluded](/faq/what-is-included-in-a-new-low-rise-home).
- **Deposit structure**, amount and schedule, which is often more negotiable than price.
- **Levy caps**, whether offered at launch and at what figure. DECO has published $0; Arista and OPUS have not published theirs.
- **Delivery record**, each builder's history on closing dates. [How to assess a builder](/guides/builder-due-diligence-guide).

Same community, same streets, materially different deals. That comparison is the single most useful thing to do here, and it is difficult from a price list alone.
