## What's being built in Toronto

Toronto has very little new freehold low-rise. The city is built out and the planning framework favours density, so what appears is infill townhomes on former commercial sites rather than subdivisions. If you want new freehold within the city boundary, supply is measured in a handful of projects a year.

Communities that have launched or are actively selling here include:

- 180 Towns (Icon/Dash, Newtonbrook)
- The Woodside Collection (95 Developments, Agincourt)
- NY Walk Towns (Menkes, Lansing-Westgate)

**Builders active in Toronto:** Icon Homes, Dash Developments, Menkes, 95 Developments.

## What the rebates are worth at local prices

New low-rise here currently runs roughly **$929,000 to $1,450,000**, and that band decides your rebate position. Here is the arithmetic at both ends.

| | At $929,000 | At $1,450,000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario ENHR (no first-time buyer test) | **$74,320** | **$80,000** |
| Federal rebate (first-time buyers only) | **$46,450** | **$5,000** |

At the entry end, a home at $929,000 sits **below $1,000,000**, which means the **full 8%** Ontario rebate, $74,320, plus the full 5% federal rebate for a first-time buyer. This is the most favourable band in the whole structure.

The band here straddles **$1,000,000**, which is where the structure changes: above it the Ontario rebate stops growing and sits flat at **$80,000**, while the federal rebate begins tapering toward zero at $1,500,000. Two buyers in the same community can be in different places.

If you have **owned a home before**, note that the Ontario rebate still applies to you, it has no first-time buyer test, and move-up buyers routinely assume otherwise and claim nothing. [Read this one](/faq/do-i-have-to-be-first-time-buyer-ontario-rebate).


These are illustrative maximums at the ends of the local range, not a quote. [Run your own numbers](/hst-rebate/checker).

## Land transfer tax, the Toronto penalty

Buying inside the City of Toronto means paying land transfer tax **twice**: the Ontario tax and the City of Toronto municipal land transfer tax, on broadly similar bands.

On a $1,100,000 home that is roughly **$18,475 provincially and about the same again municipally**, call it **$37,000** against roughly $18,475 for the identical home in Vaughan or Markham. First-time buyers can claim up to $4,000 provincially and up to $4,475 from the city, for $8,475 combined.

That gap is one of the strongest financial arguments for buying new low-rise in the 905. [How land transfer tax works](/faq/land-transfer-tax-new-home-ontario).

## Getting around

The reason people pay the premium, subway, GO, and no commute to build a life around.

Worth being honest with yourself about this. New low-rise communities are car-dependent at launch almost without exception, the retail, transit and services that make an area convenient arrive years after the homes do, and sometimes differently than the plan showed.

## Before you sign anything in Toronto

Freehold purchases in Ontario have **no cooling-off period**. The ten-day rescission right buyers have heard about is a condominium provision and does not apply to a detached home, semi or freehold townhome. Once the builder accepts your agreement, you are bound. [This is the one to understand](/faq/cooling-off-period-freehold-ontario).

So the protection has to be in place beforehand:

- **Have a lawyer read the agreement before you sign**, not after. [What they look for](/faq/lawyer-review-agreement-pre-construction)
- **Ask for a cap on development charges and levies**, the most valuable amendment available on a closing years out. [Why](/faq/development-charges-levies-cap)
- **Confirm your rebate eligibility** rather than assuming it. If your price is quoted net of the rebate and you do not qualify, the builder bills you at closing. [How that works](/faq/builder-keeps-hst-rebate-assignment)
- **Check your deposit against Tarion's limits**, freehold protection caps at 10% of price up to $100,000. [Deposit protection](/faq/is-my-deposit-protected-freehold-ontario)
- **Register for representation before your first sales centre visit.** The builder budgets the commission either way. [Why it costs you nothing](/faq/does-it-cost-more-to-use-a-realtor-precon)
