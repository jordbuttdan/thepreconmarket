Most pre-construction advice online is written about condominiums, because that is where the volume and the marketing budgets are. Low-rise is a different transaction, and some of the differences cut strongly in your favour while others cut against you.

This site is about low-rise only, **freehold detached homes, townhomes and semis**. Here is what actually changes.

## What freehold means

You own the **land and the building** in fee simple, on your own title. No condominium corporation, no declaration, no shared ownership of the structure, no monthly maintenance fee.

Everything is also yours to maintain: roof, windows, cladding, furnace, driveway, lawn. Nobody is accumulating a reserve fund for your shingles. [More](/faq/what-is-a-freehold-townhome).

## Where low-rise wins

**No interim occupancy.** Condo buyers often spend months, sometimes more than a year, paying occupancy fees of $2,500 to $4,000 a month on a unit they do not yet own, building no equity. Freehold has a **single closing**: title, mortgage and keys on the same day. On a six-month gap that is roughly **$18,000** that simply never arises. [More](/faq/occupancy-vs-final-closing-freehold).

**No monthly condo fee.** Over a decade, a $450 monthly fee is $54,000, before the increases.

**Generally stronger resale.** In most GTA markets detached leads, then semis and end units, then interior towns, then back-to-back towns.

**Land.** You own a piece of ground, which is the part that has historically appreciated.

**The rebates apply equally.** All low-rise types qualify for the same [federal and Ontario programs](/guides/hst-rebate-guide-ontario-new-home), worth up to roughly $130,000, and a townhome under $1,000,000 receives the **full** 8% ENHR rather than the flat band.

## Where low-rise loses

**No cooling-off period.** This is the big one. Condominium buyers get a 10-day statutory rescission right. Freehold buyers get **none**, the agreement binds on acceptance. [Why the difference](/faq/condo-vs-freehold-cooling-off).

So a condo buyer can sign Saturday and have a lawyer review Monday. You cannot. [Reverse the order](/faq/lawyer-review-agreement-pre-construction).

**Less deposit protection, structurally.** Condo deposits get trust protections under the Condominium Act *in addition* to Tarion coverage. Freehold deposits do not have that second layer, and Tarion caps at 10% of price up to **$100,000**. [Check your exposure](/faq/is-my-deposit-protected-freehold-ontario).

**Higher closing costs.** Development levies, utility and service connections, meter installations, grading deposits, commonly **$30,000 to $45,000** on a $1.1M home outside Toronto. [Breakdown](/guides/pre-construction-closing-cost-guide-ontario).

**More excluded from the base price.** Appliances, window coverings, fencing, rear sod, deck. Often another $25,000 to $60,000. [What's not included](/faq/what-is-included-in-a-new-low-rise-home).

**You wait for the outside.** Sod, final grading and the driveway top coat typically come after you move in, sometimes a season or two later. [Timelines](/faq/grading-sodding-driveway-timeline).

## The POTL question

Many newer low-rise communities are **POTL**, your freehold lot is tied to a small common elements condominium corporation maintaining private laneways, visitor parking or a park block. Expect roughly $75 to $250 a month, far less than a condo fee.

It also has a legal consequence worth knowing: because the purchase includes an interest in a common elements condominium corporation, a rescission right **may** be engaged where that interest comes from the declarant. If your agreement mentions a POTL, ask your lawyer **immediately**, it may be the only exit a low-rise buyer ever gets. [More](/faq/potl-common-elements-condo-fees).

## Choosing between the types

The steps are uneven. Interior town to end unit or semi is often $40,000 to $80,000; semi to detached is usually much more. That makes **end units and semis the value sweet spot** for many buyers, most of the light, a side yard, better resale, without the detached premium. [Comparison](/faq/detached-vs-semi-vs-townhome-precon).

## The lot is the decision

In low-rise, the lot cannot be changed and the finishes can. A walkout basement, protected land behind you, or genuinely wider frontage are permanent advantages. Three extra feet of grass is not. [How to choose](/guides/choosing-a-lot-new-low-rise).
