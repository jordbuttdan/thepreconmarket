Use this before you sign, not after. On a freehold home in Ontario there is **no cooling-off period** — the agreement binds the moment the builder accepts it, so a review afterward only tells you what you are already stuck with.

Print it, take it to your lawyer, and work down the list together.

## The two items that matter most

If you only check two things, make them these.

**The HST clause.** Most Ontario builder agreements state the price inclusive of HST and require you to assign the rebate to the builder at closing. That is normal. What matters is the indemnity: if you turn out not to qualify, you owe the builder the rebate amount **on closing day**, in certified funds. On a low-rise home that is up to $80,000. [How this works](/faq/builder-keeps-hst-rebate-assignment).

**The levy cap.** Development charges rise between signing and a closing three or four years out, and the standard clause passes the increase to you with no ceiling. A cap converts an unknown into a budgetable number. It is the most valuable amendment available and the one buyers most often forget to ask for. [Why](/faq/development-charges-levies-cap).

## One you will not think to ask for

**Assignment rights.** Most low-rise agreements restrict or prohibit assignment. Asking for the right at signing costs the builder very little; asking for it two years later, when your circumstances have changed and you need out, is close to impossible. It is the main escape hatch a pre-construction buyer ever gets. [More](/faq/assignment-sale-freehold-low-rise).
