No. This is the most common misconception in pre-construction, and it costs unrepresented buyers the benefit of representation for no saving at all.

## How builders actually budget

A builder's pro forma includes a line for **sales and marketing**, and within it a co-operating brokerage commission, the amount paid to a broker who brings a buyer. That line is set before a single home is sold, and the price list is built on top of it.

The commission is therefore **already inside the price you are quoted**, in exactly the same way the sales centre, the model home, the renderings and the advertising are.

## What happens if you come alone

Nothing good, financially. The builder does not hand you the commission as a discount. There is no unrepresented price list. You pay the posted price and the builder keeps the amount they had budgeted to pay a broker.

Occasionally a builder will offer an unrepresented buyer a small incentive. It is almost never the size of the commission, and it is usually a décor credit rather than a price reduction.

## Why builders prefer co-operating brokers anyway

It is worth understanding that this is not a loophole you are exploiting, builders actively want broker business:

- Brokers bring **qualified** buyers, reducing fall-through.
- Brokers handle buyer questions and expectations, lowering the builder's cost to serve.
- Brokers bring **repeat** volume across phases and projects.

That is why platinum and VIP allocations exist, and why they go to brokers rather than to the public. [How that works](/faq/what-is-platinum-access-pre-construction).

## What you are actually choosing between

Since the price is the same either way, the choice is:

**Unrepresented**, you negotiate alone, against a professional whose duty is to the vendor, with no independent view of the market, the builder's record or which amendments are achievable.

**Represented**, the same price, with someone whose obligation is to you.

Put that way, buying unrepresented is not a cost saving. It is giving something away.

## The honest caveat

Representation is only worth having if the person is actually useful. A broker who simply walks you into a sales centre and collects the commission has added nothing. What you should expect is the market context, the builder intelligence, the agreement read commercially, and a clear view of which lots and which phases are worth it. [What good representation does](/faq/do-i-need-a-realtor-pre-construction).

Ask what someone will actually do for you, and judge the answer.
