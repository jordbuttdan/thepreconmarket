Yes, and it is common — buyers relocating to the GTA for work, families buying for a child attending university here, and Canadians returning from abroad.

The practical obstacles are mostly about deposits, taxes and the rebate conditions.

## If you are moving to Ontario from another province

Largely straightforward. You are a Canadian resident, so the usual deposit structure and mortgage process apply.

Two points to watch:

- **The [primary residence requirement](/faq/hst-rebate-primary-residence-requirement)** for the HST rebates. If you intend to live there on arrival, fine. If you will rent it out until you move, that is a [rental purchase](/faq/hst-rebate-rental-investment-property) and the owner-occupied rebates do not apply.
- **The [federal first-time buyer test](/faq/spouse-owned-home-first-time-buyer)** looks at homes you or your spouse owned and lived in **anywhere in the world** — not just Ontario, not just Canada.

## If you are not a resident of Canada

More complicated, and the position has shifted repeatedly in recent years. Expect:

- **Larger deposits.** Builders commonly require 20% to 35% from non-resident purchasers, on a faster schedule.
- **Different mortgage terms.** Fewer lenders, larger down payments, more documentation.
- **Additional taxes.** Ontario applies a speculation tax on certain residential purchases by non-residents, and federal restrictions on residential purchases by non-Canadians have been in force with various exemptions and end dates.

That last point is the one to be careful about. These rules have changed more than once and the exemptions are technical. **Do not rely on any website, including this one, for your eligibility to purchase.** Get current advice from a real estate lawyer before you commit anything, because the agreement will be firm on signing and [there is no cooling-off period on freehold](/faq/cooling-off-period-freehold-ontario).

## The rebate position for non-residents

The enhanced rebates require the home to be the **primary place of residence** of the buyer or a **relation**, and the buyer to be the first occupant. A home bought from abroad and left vacant, or rented out, does not meet that.

The federal first-time buyer rebate additionally requires the buyer to be a **Canadian citizen or permanent resident**.

A parent purchasing for a child who will live in the home may be in a better position, since "relation" can satisfy the occupancy limb — but whether a particular relationship qualifies is a specific legislative question. Confirm it with an accountant before assuming a rebate worth tens of thousands.

## Practicalities

- **Signing from abroad.** Builders vary on electronic signatures and couriered drafts; some require attendance. Ask early.
- **Bank drafts.** The first deposit is usually a draft on signing, which needs a Canadian account or a workable arrangement.
- **Powers of attorney** for closing, which your lawyer prepares in advance.
- **Someone to attend the [pre-delivery inspection](/faq/pre-delivery-inspection-pdi).** Do not skip it or let the builder rush it because you are overseas — it is the warranty baseline.
