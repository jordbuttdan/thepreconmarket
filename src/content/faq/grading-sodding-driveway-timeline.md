You will very likely move into a house with a dirt yard and a gravel driveway. This is normal, it is in your agreement, and it still surprises almost everyone.

## Why it waits

**Final grading needs the neighbours finished.** Lot grading is engineered across the whole block so water drains to the street or a swale. Your lot cannot be finalised while the lot next door is still a construction site with heavy equipment crossing it.

**Municipal inspection is required.** The municipality inspects grading against the approved plan before signing off. That is a queue, and it moves with the subdivision rather than with your house.

**Sod and asphalt are seasonal.** Neither goes down in winter. A December closing means spring at the earliest, and if the block is not ready by then, the following season.

**Driveways are often paved base-first**, with the top coat applied a year or more later once settling has occurred, which is better practice, not a shortcut.

## Realistic timelines

- **Spring or summer closing:** sod and base paving sometimes within a few months, if the block is complete.
- **Fall or winter closing:** expect the following spring or summer.
- **Early phase of a community:** longer. Possibly a second season.
- **Final top coat on the driveway:** commonly 1–2 years after closing.

## The deposit you pay for it

Your [statement of adjustments](/faq/adjustments-on-closing-new-build) will likely include a **grading and sodding deposit**, often $1,000 to $3,000. It is usually refundable once the work is done and inspected.

Two things to do: **keep the receipt**, and **diarise a reminder**. These refunds are not always issued automatically, and a buyer who never asks sometimes never receives it. Follow up at the one-year mark.

## What you must not do

**Do not alter the grading.** Not a garden bed against the foundation, not a patio, not regrading a low spot, not a fence that interrupts a swale. Altering grading before final approval can:

- Void the grading portion of your [Tarion coverage](/faq/tarion-warranty-coverage-new-home).
- Make you responsible for drainage problems, including a neighbour's.
- Cause the municipality to refuse sign-off and the builder to withhold your deposit.

Wait for approval. Then landscape.

## Record it at the PDI

At the [pre-delivery inspection](/faq/pre-delivery-inspection-pdi), **write down the state of the exterior**, no sod, gravel driveway, rough grade, no walkway. Photograph it. That establishes what was outstanding at handover, which is exactly what the form is for.

## Living with it

Budget for the gap. You may want interim gravel or mulch, a temporary walkway, and a plan for mud by the back door. Plan your landscaping, fencing and deck for **after** final grading approval, building before it is how people lose both a deposit and a warranty claim.
