The honest answer for a freehold buyer is: usually no, and the exceptions are narrow.

## Your actual exits

**A condition in your agreement.** If you negotiated a financing or other condition and it has not been waived, you may be able to walk within its terms. Most builder agreements for low-rise contain no such condition unless you asked. [More](/faq/conditional-financing-pre-construction).

**A rescission right, if one applies.** Condominium purchases carry the 10-day statutory right. A [freehold home with a POTL](/faq/potl-common-elements-condo-fees) may engage one depending on how it is structured — ask your lawyer immediately if that is you, because the window is short.

**Builder default.** If the builder fails to meet its obligations — missing critical dates beyond what the agreement and Tarion permit, for instance — you may acquire a right to terminate. This is a legal determination on your documents, not a judgment call you make yourself.

**Mutual release.** The builder agrees to let you out. This happens, particularly in a market where they can resell the lot for more than you agreed to pay, and it usually costs you something. It is a negotiation, not a right.

## What is not an exit

- Rates went up and you no longer like the deal.
- Your home did not sell.
- You found something better.
- You were told you had ten days and you did not.
- Your income changed and you cannot qualify for the mortgage.

That last one is the painful one, and it is common on long low-rise closings. Not being able to close is a **breach**, not a cancellation. [What happens then](/faq/deposit-lost-if-cannot-close).

## If you want out, do this in order

1. **Get your agreement to a real estate lawyer today.** Not next week. If any rescission or condition right exists, it is time-limited.
2. **Do not stop your deposit cheques** on your own initiative. Missing a scheduled deposit is itself a default and weakens your position.
3. **Do not tell the builder you intend to walk** before you have advice. It changes the negotiation.
4. **Ask about the alternatives** — [assignment](/faq/assignment-sale-freehold-low-rise) where the agreement permits it, or a negotiated release — which are often better outcomes than a breach.

## The uncomfortable context

Builders sell low-rise in timed releases, with the lot you want available for a weekend. That pressure is deliberate and it is effective. It also means a meaningful number of people sign agreements they have not read, for homes they are not certain they can fund, with no exit.
