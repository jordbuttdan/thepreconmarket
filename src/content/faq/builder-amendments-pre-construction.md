The builder's agreement is a standard form. Amendments are the documents attached to it that change its terms for your specific purchase — and they are where most of the real negotiation in pre-construction happens.

## What amendments typically cover

- **Capping development charges and levies** at a stated dollar figure.
- **Confirming incentives** — a décor allowance, free upgrades, an appliance package, a reduced deposit schedule.
- **Adjusting the deposit schedule** — different amounts or dates.
- **Assignment rights**, where the standard agreement restricts them.
- **Confirming specific inclusions** that were promised verbally at the sales centre.

That last category matters more than people realise. **A promise made by a sales representative and not written into an amendment does not exist.** If it is not on paper, it is not part of your purchase, however sincerely it was offered.

## What is actually negotiable

Less than buyers hope, more than builders imply, and it depends almost entirely on the project's sales position:

**Early release, strong demand.** Very little moves. The builder has more buyers than lots. You will get the standard package and the posted incentives.

**Mid-phase, steady sales.** Levy caps are often available. Décor dollars sometimes. Deposit timing occasionally.

**Slow phase, or inventory sitting.** Considerably more is available — larger caps, meaningful upgrade allowances, extended deposits, and sometimes price.

This is why knowing where a project actually sits matters more than negotiating technique. The same request lands differently in two different months.

## The amendment worth asking for first

If you ask for one thing, make it a **cap on development charges and levies**. On a low-rise closing three or four years out, municipal charges can rise materially, and an uncapped clause passes that increase to you with no ceiling. A cap converts an unknown into a number you can budget. [More on levies](/faq/development-charges-levies-cap).

A décor allowance is more fun. A levy cap is worth more.

## How to handle it

1. **Get the standard agreement and all amendments before you sign** — the amendments are part of the deal, not paperwork.
2. **Have them reviewed together.** An amendment can undo a protection elsewhere in the agreement.
3. **Confirm every verbal promise appears in writing.**
4. **Know the project's sales position** before deciding what to ask for.

That last point is the part a buyer cannot easily do alone, and it is a large part of what representation adds — knowing which phase is moving, which builder is flexible this quarter, and what other buyers at the same project actually got.
