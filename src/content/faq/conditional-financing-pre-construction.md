Usually not — and understanding why reframes how you should approach the whole purchase.

## Why builders refuse

A resale seller granting a five-day financing condition is taking a short, bounded risk. A builder being asked to grant a financing condition on a home closing in 2030 is being asked to hold a lot for years against a mortgage approval that cannot meaningfully be given today. No lender will commit to funding a purchase three or four years out.

So the standard builder agreement for new low-rise is **firm on signing**. Combined with [the absence of any cooling-off period](/faq/cooling-off-period-freehold-ontario), that means a freehold pre-construction purchase is typically binding and unconditional from the moment it is accepted.

## What you get instead

Builders generally require a **mortgage pre-qualification letter** from a lender or broker, often within a set number of days of signing. It is important not to misread what that is:

- It satisfies the **builder** that you are a plausible buyer.
- It does **not** protect you. It is not a condition, and failing to obtain final approval later is not an escape.

A pre-approval today is an assessment of today's income, today's rates and today's guidelines, for a closing that is years away. [Why that gap matters](/faq/mortgage-approval-closing-years-later).

## Where a condition is sometimes possible

Not never:

- **Slow inventory.** A builder carrying unsold completed or near-complete homes has more reason to accommodate you.
- **Short closings.** On an inventory home closing in 60 or 90 days, a financing condition is a normal ask because a lender can actually underwrite it.
- **Specific negotiated amendments.** Occasionally a deposit-structure or qualification amendment achieves part of the same comfort.

Where you can get one, it is worth more than any décor allowance on offer.

## How to protect yourself without a condition

Since the contract will not protect you, the protection has to be financial:

1. **Qualify conservatively.** Model the purchase at a materially higher rate than today's, because you are buying the right to borrow at an unknown future rate.
2. **Do not count on income growth** to make it work.
3. **Keep the deposit schedule fundable** from money you actually have, not money you expect.
4. **Budget the [closing costs](/faq/closing-costs-new-construction-ontario)** — land transfer tax, levies, adjustments — on top of the deposit. They are commonly tens of thousands.
5. **Know your [HST position](/faq/builder-keeps-hst-rebate-assignment)**, because a failed rebate is a closing-day bill.
6. **Keep the lender relationship alive** through the build, and revisit annually.
