Lot premiums are the biggest discretionary decision in a low-rise purchase, and the one buyers get least guidance on.

## What gets a premium

- **Wider frontage**, a 42-foot lot where the base model sits on a 36.
- **Corner lots**, more light, more windows, often more exposed.
- **Walkout or lookout basements**, grade allowing a basement door or full windows.
- **Backing onto a park, ravine, pond or protected land**, no rear neighbour.
- **Pie-shaped lots** with a wider rear yard.
- **Cul-de-sac or premium street positions.**

Premiums commonly run $10,000 to $60,000 and reach well past $150,000 on a ravine lot in a strong community.

## Which premiums hold their value

The test is whether the advantage is **permanent and scarce**.

**Usually worth it:**

- **A walkout basement.** It changes what the house *is*, an accessible lower level, light, and potentially a legal secondary suite. It cannot be added later. Frequently the best-value premium on offer.
- **Backing onto protected land.** Permanently no rear neighbour. Ravine, park, conservation. The scarcity is structural and resale buyers pay for it.
- **Meaningfully wider frontage**, 36 to 42 feet is a real difference in the house that fits on it.

**Often not worth it:**

- **Small frontage steps.** 30 to 32 feet. You pay for three feet of grass.
- **Corner lots**, which are genuinely divisive. More light and a side yard, but more sidewalk to shovel, more exposure, and often more restrictive siting. Some resale buyers discount them.
- **Backing onto a future park** that is drawn on a plan but not built or guaranteed.
- **Pie-shaped lots** where the extra area is unusable.

## The tax interaction nobody mentions

This matters in the GTA. Lot premiums add to your purchase price, and your purchase price determines your rebate band.

A $1,450,000 base price with a $60,000 ravine premium is a $1,510,000 purchase, which has crossed the **$1,500,000 federal cliff** where the first-time buyer GST rebate drops to **zero**. The premium may have cost you more in lost rebate than the premium itself. [The cliffs](/faq/hst-rebate-over-1-5-million).

Exactly how premiums and upgrades count toward consideration for rebate purposes depends on how they are documented in your agreement, a question for your lawyer and accountant on your paperwork. But the time to **ask** is before you select the lot, not after.

## How to decide

1. **Visit the site** if you can. Grades, sightlines and what is actually behind the lot are not legible from a plan.
2. **Check what "backing onto green space" means.** Get the zoning of the land behind it, not the rendering.
3. **Ask what the premium was in the previous phase.** Premiums escalate; sometimes sharply.
4. **Model your all-in price against the rebate bands** before committing. [Checker](/hst-rebate/checker).
5. **Ask whether it will resell.** A premium only pays back if the next buyer values it too.

That last judgment is the hardest to make alone, and it is where market experience earns its keep. [Ask before you choose the lot](/book).
