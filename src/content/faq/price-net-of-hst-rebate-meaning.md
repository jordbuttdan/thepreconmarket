"Net of HST rebate" is a pricing convention, and it is the reason two buyers can look at the same floor plan and have completely different ideas of what it costs.

## What you are looking at

When a builder advertises a townhome at $899,990 "net of HST rebate," they mean: the HST is included, and we have already deducted the rebate we expect to collect on your behalf. The number on the price list is the after-rebate number.

It is not a discount you still have to earn. It is a discount **already applied**, conditional on you qualifying.

## Why that matters

Because the conditional part is real. If you do not qualify — you fail the [first-time buyer test](/faq/spouse-owned-home-first-time-buyer), you decide to [rent it out](/faq/hst-rebate-rental-investment-property), you are not the first occupant — the builder loses the rebate they priced in and recovers it from you at closing.

On an Ontario low-rise home, the amounts in play are large:

- Federal first-time buyer rebate: up to $50,000
- Ontario ENHR: up to $80,000

So "net of HST rebate" can conceal a six-figure contingent liability sitting quietly in your purchase price.

## What to ask at the sales centre

Three questions, and get the answers in writing:

1. **Is this price net of the HST rebate, and if so, what rebate amount is assumed?** You want the dollar figure they built in, not a reassurance.
2. **Which programs is that figure assuming?** Federal only, ENHR only, or both.
3. **What is the price if I do not qualify?** That is your real worst-case number, and it is the one your mortgage needs to survive.

## Comparing projects honestly

This is also why price comparison between projects is harder than it looks. One builder quotes net of rebate, another quotes gross, and the list prices are not comparable until you normalise them. On a $1M home the gap can be $80,000 or more — larger than most of the differences buyers agonise over.

If you are weighing specific projects, [run both numbers](/hst-rebate/checker) and compare like with like.
