New construction closing costs are materially higher than resale, because a builder passes through a list of items a private seller never charges you for.

## The list

On a $1,100,000 freehold home outside Toronto:

| Item | Typical | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontario land transfer tax | ~$18,475 | Less up to $4,000 first-time buyer rebate |
| Toronto municipal LTT | ~$18,475 | **City of Toronto only** |
| Development charges / levies | $5,000 – $20,000+ | Uncapped unless you negotiated a cap |
| Utility and service connections | $1,500 – $3,500 | Water, gas, hydro, meters |
| Tarion enrolment fee | ~$600 – $1,500 | Scales with price |
| Legal fees and disbursements | $2,500 – $4,000 | Higher than resale; longer document set |
| Grading / sodding / driveway deposits | $1,000 – $3,000 | Often refundable later |
| Title insurance | $400 – $900 | |
| Property tax and utility adjustments | Varies | Reimbursing the builder's prepayments |
| Occupancy or interim fees | Usually none on freehold | A condo item |

Realistic total outside Toronto: **$30,000 to $45,000**. Inside Toronto, add roughly $18,000.

## The two that can run away from you

**Development charges.** Municipal charges rise, and a standard builder clause passes increases through to you with no ceiling. On a closing three or four years out this is a genuinely open-ended item. [Negotiate a cap](/faq/development-charges-levies-cap). It is the single most valuable amendment to ask for.

**A failed HST rebate.** If your price was [net of HST rebate](/faq/price-net-of-hst-rebate-meaning) and you do not qualify, the builder bills the rebate amount at closing, up to **$80,000**. This dwarfs every other line above. [Confirm your eligibility](/hst-rebate/checker) long before closing.

## Not a closing cost, but due at the same time

**An appraisal shortfall.** If the home appraises below your purchase price, the gap is cash, on closing day. [Why this happens](/faq/mortgage-approval-closing-years-later).

## What your lawyer will tell you, and when

You receive a **statement of adjustments** shortly before closing itemising all of it. "Shortly" is often a week or two, which is not enough time to raise money, which is why this needs budgeting years earlier. [More on adjustments](/faq/adjustments-on-closing-new-build).

Ask your lawyer for an **estimate** of the statement several months out. Most will provide one, and it converts a nasty surprise into a plan.
