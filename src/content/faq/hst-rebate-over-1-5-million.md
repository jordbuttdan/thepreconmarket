There are two separate price cliffs, and they are in different places. This matters a lot in markets like Oakville, King City, Kleinburg and Angus Glen, where new detached homes routinely clear both.

## The federal cliff: $1,500,000

The federal first-time buyer GST rebate phases out on a straight line between $1,000,000 and $1,500,000. At $1,499,000 there is a small amount left. At $1,500,000 it is **zero**, and it stays zero.

## The Ontario cliff: $1,850,000

The ENHR is more generous at the top end:

| Price | ENHR |
| --- | --- |
| Up to $1,000,000 | Full 8% |
| $1,000,000 – $1,500,000 | Flat $80,000 |
| $1,500,000 – $1,850,000 | Partial, declining |
| $1,850,000+ | Nothing, standard rebate only |

So a $1,700,000 home gets no federal rebate but still gets meaningful provincial relief. A $1,900,000 home drops to the [$24,000 standard Ontario rebate](/faq/hst-rebate-24000-standard) and nothing else.

## Where the cliffs actually bite

The practical problem in low-rise is that **you often do not control which side of the line you land on.** Base price is one thing; [lot premiums](/faq/lot-premiums-explained) and [upgrades](/faq/builder-upgrades-worth-it) are another. A $1,450,000 base price with a $60,000 corner-lot premium and $40,000 of structural upgrades is a $1,550,000 purchase, and may have crossed a cliff that costs more than the upgrades did.

Whether a premium or upgrade counts toward the consideration for rebate purposes depends on how it is documented in the agreement. This is a question for your lawyer and accountant on your specific paperwork, not something to resolve from a web page, but it is a question worth **asking before you select finishes**, not after.

## The practical advice

If your purchase is landing anywhere near $1,000,000, $1,500,000 or $1,850,000, model the rebate at your all-in price including premiums and upgrades, not at the base price on the price list. [The checker](/hst-rebate/checker) will show you which band you are in, and a conversation before you finalise your selections is cheaper than one after.
