This is the central financial risk of pre-construction, and it is structural rather than unlucky. You are committing to a purchase today and arranging the funding years from now.

## What gets re-tested before closing

Roughly three to six months before your closing date, a lender underwrites the deal on the facts as they then stand:

- **Your income and employment.** A job change, self-employment, parental leave or retirement can all alter what you qualify for.
- **Your credit and other debts.** A car lease or a new line of credit taken out in year two reduces capacity in year four.
- **Rates and the qualifying stress test.** Higher rates mean a smaller mortgage for the same income.
- **Lender guidelines**, which change.
- **The appraised value of the home.**

## The appraisal gap

This is the one that hurts most, because it is outside your control entirely.

Lenders lend against the **lesser of purchase price and appraised value**. If you agreed to $1,200,000 in 2026 and the home appraises at $1,100,000 at closing, the lender's 80% is calculated on $1,100,000 — $880,000, not $960,000.

That $80,000 difference is **yours to fund in cash, at closing**, on top of everything else. Your deposit does not stretch to cover it.

In a rising market this never comes up. In a flat or falling one it is common, and buyers who put 10% down are the most exposed: a 10% decline can erase the entire equity position and leave a shortfall.

## There is no exit

Worth repeating, because the combination is what causes real damage:

- [No cooling-off period on freehold](/faq/cooling-off-period-freehold-ontario).
- [Generally no financing condition](/faq/conditional-financing-pre-construction).
- Failing to close is a **breach**, exposing your deposit and potentially more. [What happens](/faq/deposit-lost-if-cannot-close).

## How to protect yourself

**Buy below your maximum.** The single most effective step. Headroom absorbs rate moves and appraisal gaps.

**Model a stressed scenario before signing.** Higher rate, flat value, no income growth. If it does not work on those assumptions, it is not a safe purchase.

**Keep a cash reserve beyond the deposit** specifically for an appraisal gap and [closing costs](/faq/closing-costs-new-construction-ontario).

**Do not add debt during the build.** Treat your borrowing capacity as spoken for.

**Review with your lender annually.** Problems found early have solutions. Problems found at closing have consequences.

**Understand your [HST position](/faq/builder-keeps-hst-rebate-assignment)** — a failed rebate assignment adds up to $80,000 to closing-day cash.
