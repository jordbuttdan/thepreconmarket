No, and this is one of the genuine financial advantages of buying low-rise rather than a condominium.

## What interim occupancy is, and why condos have it

A new condominium building becomes habitable floor by floor, but **title cannot transfer** until the condominium corporation is registered, which happens after the whole building is complete and the municipality and land registry have done their part.

That creates a gap. The condo buyer is allowed to move in and occupy the unit during it, but does not own anything yet. During **interim occupancy** they pay the builder a monthly **occupancy fee** roughly comprising notional interest on the unpaid purchase price, estimated taxes and estimated common expenses.

That money is not a mortgage payment and builds no equity. It is often described as "phantom rent," and on a downtown condo the gap can run many months, occasionally more than a year, at $2,500 to $4,000 a month.

## Why freehold does not have it

A freehold home is a complete parcel with its own title. There is no corporation to register and nothing to wait for. When the home is finished and the municipality permits occupancy, **you close**: title transfers, your mortgage funds, you get the keys, and you start paying a mortgage on a home you own.

One closing. No occupancy period. No occupancy fees.

## What this is worth

On a comparable purchase, avoiding six months of occupancy fees at $3,000 a month is **$18,000** that buys you nothing in a condo and simply does not arise on a freehold home. It rarely features in the comparison buyers make between a condo and a townhome, and it should.

It also simplifies the whole end of the transaction: one date to plan around, one set of funds, one move. [Delays still happen](/faq/closing-delays-pre-construction-ontario), but you are not paying to live somewhere you do not yet own while they resolve.

## The one freehold wrinkle

If your freehold home sits on a **POTL** tied to a common elements condominium corporation, there is a monthly fee for the shared elements, private road, visitor parking, snow clearing on common areas. That is an ongoing maintenance fee, **not** an occupancy fee, and it is usually modest. [More on POTLs](/faq/potl-common-elements-condo-fees).

## What you do pay at closing

A freehold closing has no occupancy fees but it does have a substantial list of [adjustments and levies](/faq/adjustments-on-closing-new-build), typically $30,000 to $45,000 all in on a $1.1M home outside Toronto. [Full breakdown](/faq/closing-costs-new-construction-ontario).
