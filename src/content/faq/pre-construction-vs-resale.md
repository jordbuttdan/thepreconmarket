Honest answer: it depends on your circumstances, and anyone who tells you pre-construction is always better is selling something.

## Where pre-construction genuinely wins

**The rebates, right now.** An eligible buyer can currently recover up to roughly **$130,000** in combined federal and Ontario relief on a new home — and the [Ontario ENHR window closes March 31, 2027](/faq/hst-rebate-deadline-march-2027). Nothing comparable exists on resale. This is the strongest argument for buying new in the current window, and it is temporary.

**Time to assemble funds.** Deposits spread over 12 to 24 months, and closing two to four years out. For a buyer who is saving or expecting an inheritance or a maturing investment, that runway is real.

**A new home with warranty.** Seven-year structural, two-year systems, one-year workmanship. No deferred maintenance, no 30-year-old furnace. [Coverage](/faq/tarion-warranty-coverage-new-home).

**Choice of lot, model and finishes** — within limits.

**No competing offers.** You pay the price list rather than bidding against five other buyers.

## Where resale wins

**You see what you are buying.** The actual house, the actual street, the actual neighbours, the trees grown in.

**Certainty of financing.** You can make the offer conditional on financing and close in 60 to 90 days with a rate you have locked. Pre-construction offers [neither](/faq/conditional-financing-pre-construction).

**You can walk away.** A conditional resale offer has an exit. A freehold pre-construction agreement [does not](/faq/cooling-off-period-freehold-ontario).

**No construction risk.** No delays, no [builder failure](/faq/builder-cancels-project-ontario), no appraisal gap three years out.

**Established infrastructure.** Schools, transit, shops and parks exist rather than being planned.

**Lower closing costs.** No [development levies, connection charges or Tarion enrolment](/faq/closing-costs-new-construction-ontario) — often $10,000 to $30,000 less.

**Landscaping is done.** No waiting a season for [sod and a driveway](/faq/grading-sodding-driveway-timeline).

## The risk that decides it

The central pre-construction risk is this: **you commit at today's price and arrange financing at tomorrow's rates, against tomorrow's appraised value.** If the home appraises below your price at closing, you fund the gap in cash, with no exit. [How this happens](/faq/mortgage-approval-closing-years-later).

In a rising market that risk never materialises and pre-construction looks obviously right. In a flat or falling one it is the thing that hurts people.

## Who pre-construction suits

- Buyers with a **genuine time horizon** — not needing to move for two to four years.
- Buyers with **margin**, buying below their maximum rather than at it.
- Buyers who **qualify for the current rebates**, where up to $130,000 changes the arithmetic materially.
- Buyers who want new and will accept the timeline.

## Who should probably buy resale

- Anyone who needs to move within a year.
- Anyone stretched to their maximum qualification.
- Anyone who needs financing certainty.
- Anyone who would be badly hurt by a delay or an appraisal gap.
