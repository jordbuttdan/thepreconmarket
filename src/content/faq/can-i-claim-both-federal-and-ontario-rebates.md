Yes. HST in Ontario is 13%, a 5% federal portion and an 8% provincial portion, and the two rebate programs target different halves of it. They are not alternatives.

## How they fit together

- The **federal** First-Time Home Buyers' GST Rebate addresses the 5% federal portion, up to $50,000.
- The **Ontario ENHR** addresses the 8% provincial portion, up to $80,000.
- The **Ontario top-up** then adds a payment equivalent to the 5% federal portion, up to $50,000, but it is **reduced by any federal rebate you claim**.

That last point is where the arithmetic stops being simple addition. The top-up exists so that buyers who do not qualify federally are not left behind. If you already received $50,000 federally, the top-up is largely or entirely offset.

## A worked example

A first-time buyer purchases a new freehold townhome at $900,000, inside both windows, to live in:

- Federal GST rebate: 5% of $900,000 = **$45,000**
- Ontario ENHR: 8% of $900,000 = **$72,000**
- Ontario top-up: 5% of $900,000 is $45,000, less the $45,000 federal rebate = **$0**

Combined: **$117,000**, not $162,000. The top-up filled no gap because the federal rebate already covered that portion.

Now the same home bought by someone who has owned before:

- Federal GST rebate: **$0**, fails the first-time buyer test
- Ontario ENHR: **$72,000**, no first-time buyer test
- Ontario top-up: 5% of $900,000, nothing to offset = **$45,000**

Combined: also around **$117,000**. The design is deliberate.

## Why the headline is "$130,000"

The maximum lands near $130,000 at the top of the full-rebate band, around a $1,000,000 purchase price. Above that the federal rebate starts phasing out while the ENHR sits flat at $80,000, so the combined figure falls.

The practical takeaway: do not assume you are getting the headline number, and do not assume you are getting nothing. [Check your own numbers](/hst-rebate/checker), then have your accountant confirm before closing.
