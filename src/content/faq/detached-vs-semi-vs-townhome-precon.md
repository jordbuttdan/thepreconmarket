All three can be freehold, and all three are sold the same way in a new low-rise release. The differences that actually matter in daily life and at resale:

## The spectrum

| | Shared walls | Typical frontage | Yard | Relative price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Back-to-back town | Sides + rear | 18–22 ft | None | Lowest |
| Rear-lane / laneway town | Both sides | 18–24 ft | Small | Low |
| Traditional street town | Both sides | 18–25 ft | Modest | Low–mid |
| End-unit town | One side | 20–28 ft | Modest+ | Mid |
| Semi-detached | One side | 24–32 ft | Modest | Mid |
| Detached | None | 30–50+ ft | Full | Highest |

## What the differences mean in practice

**Noise.** The real variable, and it depends on construction rather than category. Party walls in new builds are considerably better than they were twenty years ago, but a shared wall is still a shared wall. Back-to-back towns are the most exposed, neighbours on three sides, including behind your living space.

**Light.** Interior towns get windows at the front and back only. An end unit or semi adds a side elevation, which changes the feel of the house more than the floor plan suggests. One of the better arguments for an end-unit premium.

**Outdoor space.** Back-to-back towns have essentially none. If you have small children or a dog, this is worth more honest thought than it usually gets at a sales centre.

**Parking.** Towns often have a single garage with tandem driveway parking, or rear-lane access with no driveway at all. Count the actual spaces for the actual cars you own.

**Maintenance.** Detached means more exterior and more yard to look after. Some buyers want precisely that; others are moving from a condo and do not.

## The pricing steps

In most GTA low-rise markets the increments are uneven. The step from an interior townhome to an end unit or a semi is frequently modest, sometimes $40,000 to $80,000, while the step from a semi to a detached home is usually much larger.

Which makes **end-unit and semi the value sweet spot** for many buyers: most of the light, a side yard, and meaningfully better resale than an interior town, without the detached premium.

## Resale liquidity

Roughly in the order of the table. Detached is the most liquid in nearly every GTA market. Back-to-back towns are the least, and are the most sensitive to market conditions, in a soft market they sit longest.

If there is any chance you sell within five years, that ordering matters more than the monthly payment difference.

## The tax angle

All three qualify for the same programs, the rebates depend on [price and primary residence](/faq/hst-rebate-primary-residence-requirement), not on house type. A townhome under $1,000,000 may receive the **full** 8% ENHR plus the full federal rebate, where a $1,400,000 detached home receives the flat $80,000 and a reduced federal amount.
