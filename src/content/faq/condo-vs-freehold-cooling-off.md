It comes down to which statute governs the purchase, not to price, risk or fairness.

## The condominium side

A pre-construction condominium purchase is regulated by Ontario's **Condominium Act**. That Act requires the declarant to give the purchaser a **disclosure statement** describing the proposed corporation: budget, fees, the declaration, rules, and what is being built.

Because the legislature recognised that no one can absorb that package at a sales centre, it attached a **10-day rescission period**. The clock generally starts once the purchaser has received the executed agreement and the disclosure statement, and the Condominium Authority of Ontario's Residential Condominium Buyers' Guide. Inside those ten days the purchaser may rescind for any reason, and the deposit is returned.

The right exists to make the disclosure meaningful.

## The freehold side

A freehold home, detached, semi, or freehold townhome, is not a condominium. There is no declarant, no disclosure statement, no condominium corporation, and the Condominium Act does not apply.

So there is no disclosure obligation of that kind, and correspondingly no rescission right. Your protection comes from a different place: the agreement itself, Tarion's new home warranty regime, and whatever your lawyer negotiates.

## Is that logical?

Arguably not, from a buyer's point of view. A $1.4M freehold detached home in Kleinburg is a larger financial commitment than a $600,000 condo, is sold with less mandated disclosure, and carries less statutory protection. The difference is historical and structural, not a judgment about risk.

It is simply the law as it stands, and the practical consequence for you is specific: **on freehold, all of your protection has to be built in before you sign.**

## What freehold buyers get instead

Not nothing, but different things, and none of them is a right to change your mind:

- **Tarion deposit protection**, subject to limits that may be lower than your deposit. [Check this](/faq/is-my-deposit-protected-freehold-ontario).
- **Tarion delayed closing compensation** if the builder misses dates. [More](/faq/closing-delays-pre-construction-ontario).
- **Statutory warranty coverage** after closing. [More](/faq/tarion-warranty-coverage-new-home).
- **Whatever conditions you negotiate** into the agreement.

That last one is the only one that lets you out, and it only exists if someone puts it there.

## The practical difference

A condo buyer can sign on Saturday and have a lawyer review it on Monday. A freehold buyer cannot. [Reverse the order](/faq/lawyer-review-agreement-pre-construction), lawyer first, signature second.
