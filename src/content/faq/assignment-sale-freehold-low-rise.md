An assignment is the sale of your **contractual position**, your right to buy the home, rather than the home itself, which you do not yet own.

It is the main exit available to a pre-construction buyer whose circumstances change. Whether you have it depends on paperwork you signed years earlier.

## Most low-rise agreements restrict it

Freehold builder agreements commonly:

- **Prohibit assignment entirely**, or
- Permit it **only with the builder's written consent**, which may be withheld at their discretion, and
- Charge an **assignment fee**, frequently $5,000 to $25,000 or a percentage of the price, and
- **Prohibit marketing** the assignment publicly, including on MLS or social media.

Builders restrict assignments because they do not want you competing with their own unsold inventory in the same community, and because it complicates their rebate and closing administration.

## Ask for the right at signing

This is the practical lesson, and it is cheap to act on: **negotiate assignment rights when you sign**, before you need them. A clause permitting assignment on reasonable terms costs the builder little at that moment and is close to impossible to obtain later, when you are asking because you are in difficulty and have no leverage.

Almost no buyer thinks to ask. It is one of the more valuable requests in an [amendment](/faq/builder-amendments-pre-construction).

## The tax side is not simple

Two separate issues, both real:

**The enhanced rebates.** For the [Ontario ENHR](/faq/ontario-enhanced-new-housing-rebate), the anti-avoidance rules generally require **both** the original agreement **and** the assignment to fall inside the April 2026 – March 2027 window. An assignment can therefore destroy rebate eligibility that the original agreement had, which affects what a buyer will pay you. [More](/faq/assignment-purchase-hst-rebate).

**GST/HST on the assignment itself.** The amount you receive above your original price can attract GST/HST, and the treatment depends on the structure and on your intention when you bought. Whether your gain is a capital gain or business income is a separate question with a materially different tax outcome, and CRA has taken an active interest in assignment activity.

Do not model your net proceeds without an accountant. The gross number and the net number can differ enormously.

## If you need out

Order of operations matters:

1. **Read the agreement**, or have your lawyer read it, to find out whether assignment is even available.
2. **Approach the builder** about consent, through your lawyer, before marketing anything.
3. **Price it realistically.** An assignment buyer is taking on your closing risk, your deposit schedule and possibly a worse rebate position. They discount for that.
4. **Get accounting advice** on the tax treatment before you accept an offer.
5. **Compare it against the alternatives**, a negotiated release, or [defaulting](/faq/deposit-lost-if-cannot-close), which is almost always worse.

An assignment at a small loss generally beats a default by a very wide margin. [Let's look at the options while you still have them](/book).
