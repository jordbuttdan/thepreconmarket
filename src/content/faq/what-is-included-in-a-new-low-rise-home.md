The answer is in one document: the **specifications schedule** attached to your agreement of purchase and sale. Not the model home, not the brochure, not what the sales representative said.

The model home is a marketing instrument. It is routinely built with most upgrades selected, and the signage disclosing that is routinely small.

## Generally included

- The structure, roof, exterior finish as specified
- Windows and exterior doors
- Furnace and ductwork, hot water tank (frequently **rental**, check)
- Basic electrical and plumbing to code
- Standard-grade flooring, cabinets and counters from the builder's base selections
- Standard bathroom fixtures
- Basic light fixtures, often minimal
- Base-grade trim, interior doors and hardware
- Rough grading and basic sod, eventually. [Timing](/faq/grading-sodding-driveway-timeline)
- Driveway base paving

## Frequently excluded

- **Appliances.** Often not included in low-rise base pricing at all.
- **Window coverings.** Essentially never included. Budget $3,000–$10,000.
- **Central air conditioning.** Sometimes an upgrade, sometimes included, verify.
- **Finished basement.** Almost always extra. Often a very large extra.
- **Deck, porch railings beyond code, patio.**
- **Fencing.** Usually yours, and sometimes required by the municipality.
- **Landscaping beyond basic front sod.** Rear sod is sometimes excluded.
- **Garage door opener.**
- **Pot lights**, upgraded fixtures, under-cabinet lighting.
- **Backsplash.**
- **Water softener, central vac, security system.**
- **Mirrors and some bathroom accessories.**
- **Painted or finished garage.**

## The budget gap

The exclusions add up far beyond what buyers expect. On a new freehold home, appliances, window coverings, a fence, a deck and basic rear landscaping commonly total **$25,000 to $60,000**, before anyone thinks about finishing the basement.

Combined with [closing costs of $30,000 to $45,000](/faq/closing-costs-new-construction-ontario), the real cash requirement after your deposit is considerably larger than the purchase price suggests. This is the single most common budgeting error in new low-rise.

## Questions to ask, and get answered in writing

1. **Are appliances included?** Which ones, what grade, which models.
2. **Is central air included or an upgrade?**
3. **Is the hot water tank owned or rented?** A rental is a contract you inherit.
4. **Is rear sod included, or only front?**
5. **Is fencing included?** Is it required by the municipality?
6. **What grade are the base flooring, cabinets and counters,** and can I see actual samples rather than a photograph?
7. **Is the garage door opener included?**

## Verify at the PDI

Take the specifications schedule and your upgrade selections to the [pre-delivery inspection](/faq/pre-delivery-inspection-pdi) and check the house against them line by line. Substituted or missing items are exactly what the PDI form exists to record, and an unauthorised substitution is a warranty matter.
