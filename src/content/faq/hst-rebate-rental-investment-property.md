No — and this is the single largest cash-flow surprise in Ontario pre-construction. It catches investors every single year.

## Why the owner-occupied rebates do not apply

Both the federal first-time buyer GST rebate and the Ontario ENHR require the home to be the **primary place of residence** of the buyer or a relation, and require the buyer to be the first occupant. A tenant is neither.

So if your plan is to rent the unit out, you are not eligible for the rebates the builder priced into your purchase.

## What happens at closing

Remember that your price was almost certainly quoted [net of HST rebate](/faq/price-net-of-hst-rebate-meaning), with the rebate [assigned to the builder](/faq/builder-keeps-hst-rebate-assignment). When you tell your lawyer the home is a rental:

- The assignment fails. The builder does not get the rebate.
- The builder bills you the rebate amount **at closing**.
- You pay it in certified funds on closing day, on top of your down payment, land transfer tax and adjustments.

On a $1,100,000 freehold townhome, that is up to $80,000 of provincial relief you now have to fund yourself, at the worst possible moment.

## Then you claim it back

You apply to CRA for the **New Residential Rental Property (NRRP) rebate** yourself, after closing, with a signed one-year lease and the supporting documentation. [More on the NRRP](/faq/new-residential-rental-property-rebate).

It is a real rebate and investors do receive it. But note the sequence: you **pay first and recover later**, and the recovery takes months. Builders will not assign the NRRP, because it is not theirs to assign.

## The two mistakes to avoid

**Telling the builder you will live there when you will not.** The rebate forms are sworn. Misrepresenting occupancy to obtain a rebate is a CRA matter, and your lawyer will not help you do it.

**Not budgeting for it.** Plenty of investors have had to scramble for an extra $60,000–$80,000 in the last three weeks before closing because nobody told them this. If you are buying to rent, build the gross HST into your cash-to-close from day one.
