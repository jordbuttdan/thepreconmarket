Before the enhanced programs arrived, this was the Ontario new home rebate. It still exists, it is permanent, and it is what you fall back to.

## The calculation

75% of the 8% provincial portion of the HST, to a maximum of **$24,000**. The cap is reached at a price of $400,000, so in practice almost every new home in the GTA hits the maximum, $24,000 and no more, whether the home costs $500,000 or $3,000,000.

## Who can claim it

Unlike the federal rebate, there is **no first-time buyer test**. Unlike the ENHR, there is **no temporary window**. The conditions are the familiar ones: individual buyer, new or substantially renovated home, primary place of residence for you or a relation, first occupant.

## When it is the one that applies

Three situations:

**Your agreement falls outside the ENHR window.** Signed before April 1, 2026, or after March 31, 2027. The enhanced rebate is unavailable; this one is not.

**Your purchase price is $1,850,000 or more.** The [ENHR is gone above that line](/faq/hst-rebate-over-1-5-million) and the standard rebate is what remains.

**After the enhanced programs expire.** Once the ENHR window closes on March 31, 2027, this reverts to being the Ontario rebate for new homes.

## The size of the gap

This is worth stating plainly, because it is the strongest argument for paying attention to the current window:

| | Enhanced (in window) | Standard (outside) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $900,000 townhome | $72,000 | $24,000 |
| $1,200,000 detached | $80,000 | $24,000 |

On a $1.2M low-rise home, signing inside the window versus outside it is a **$56,000 difference** in provincial relief alone, before the [federal rebate](/faq/first-time-home-buyers-gst-rebate) and the [Ontario top-up](/faq/ontario-new-home-affordability-payment) are counted.

That is not a rounding error. It is a kitchen, a finished basement and a driveway. And the only thing separating the two columns is the date on a signature.
